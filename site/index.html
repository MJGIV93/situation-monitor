          How the scores work

          Both scores run 0 to 100 and higher is worse. Every reading carries a signal from 0 to 100, which is how much concern it adds, not how big its number is. A falling stock index or a train running late has a high signal. Each reading shows its rule and arithmetic when opened. A domain takes its worst reading plus a fifth of the next two. Domains then stack by weight. Real trouble shows up in several places at once, so a domain acting alone counts at 60% of its weight. It counts in full when two other domains are also active, or when it is severe enough to stand by itself. The points under each dial show how far that score would fall if the domain went quiet.

          0 to 9 is quiet, 10 to 24 watch, 25 to 49 elevated, 50 to 74 disrupted, 75 and up severe. The numbers inside each dial's arc mark where those bands begin.

          Readings refresh every 10 minutes, and slower sources hourly. If a source stops answering, its last reading stays up. It is dimmed after 3 hours and stops counting after 24.

          Your location

          Save where you are and local notices are judged by distance from you, not just by town. A water repair within three-quarters of a mile reads as near you, and one across town reads lower. The location stays in this browser. It is never sent to the monitor or published, and the recorded history keeps the town-wide reading. Looking up an address sends it once to the US Census Bureau's address service.
